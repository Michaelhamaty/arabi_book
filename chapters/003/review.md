Tawle check: `shesh besh` (6-5) and `habbyek` (1-1) are used correctly. `Mars`
is used correctly: Abu Nabil bears off all his checkers before Michael bears off
any, and Michael's gloss `5sert mni7` fits. Playing both sides alone (`laff el
tawle w rama men el taraf el tani`) is plausible.

Texture/voice notes: Walid now reads clearly Beiruti (`hayda/hayde`, `mesh`,
`eza`, `ya3ne`, `ba3do`, `bonjour`, `merci`, `ya 3adra`, `yentor`, `be-`
prefix), and his machine theatre (`ya 7elwe`, `ye5mor metl el 3ajine`) carries
the scene. Abu Nabil now has `walak`, `ba2a`, `ya ebni`, and `ya 7aram`; his trash
talk (`El zahr 3am yebke 3leik`, `3refni bel mars`) is the chapter's best texture.
S01 is deliberately quiet, but it has bite (`El daraj shefto mni7. Kell daraje`,
the reversal `Hada el ettifa2`). I did not add filler there. No profanity was
removed. None of the characters would naturally swear in this chapter, so I
added none.

Uncertain expressions left in place:

- `talla3 kell 7jaro` for bearing off. Clear in context, but a native player may
  say `shal`/`lamm el 7jar`. Verify.
- `Yalla, bye` (Walid on the phone): an ubiquitous Beiruti loan, kept like
  `bonjour/merci`. Replace with `Yalla, ma3 el salame` if the Arabizi-only
  rule should exclude it.
- `Bravo` (Zeina), `katalog`, `model`, `internet`: established loans.
- `mokalame` vs Damascene `mkalme`; `2alatli` vs `2aletli`. Both are heard, so
  I left them.
- `Tayybin?` (Zeina, about the flatmates): plausible Damascene, but `mni7in?`
  is more common.
- `El mni7in bya5do wa2et` (Abu Nabil): intended as "good players take time to
  beat". Slightly opaque.
- `5ayyi` from a 70-year-old to a 21-year-old: common in Damascene usage, but
  verify that it fits alongside `ya ebni`.
- `Nizar Qabbani` is kept in its established Latin spelling, with `q`.
