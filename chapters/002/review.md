Texture/voice notes: S01 now has bite: the green-door joke, the bakery queue,
the bag-and-phone business, and the kitchen-shift cursing. Rami's profanity
(`yel3an abu hal telefon`, `Allah yel3an el sho8ol w ahl el sho8ol`) is mild
enough for a first meeting with a flatmate and fits his voice card. Keep it.
Inés is the strongest voice in the chapter: deadpan threats (`bedba7ak`,
`btetla3 3al shere3`), `Kezzab`, `Hada esmo sho8ol`, `3am nsalli`. Her
negation `mu` and `lissa` are technically Damascene markers. The writer uses them
as her neutral baseline, and Chapter 003 should keep the same choice rather than
switching her to `mesh`. Michael is the plainest voice in S02 (`Shilti hay?`,
`Kanet 7elwe`). That works as tired arrival listening, but give him more of his
quick joking in Chapter 003. No romance or flirtation; the warmth stays between
flatmates.

Uncertain expressions left in place:

- `7ases 7ali fey2an metl el 3afrit` (wide awake from jet lag): plausible, but
  not a fixed idiom I can vouch for.
- `el telefon shabak` (the phone got a signal): common colloquially, register unverified.
- `basta` for a stair landing: believed Levantine usage. Verify.
- `ydayy3o el 3alam` (to get people lost): believed Damascene. Verify.
- `3am tsawmini 3al akel`: haggling sense believed natural. Verify.
- `Ma sadda2et sakkarna hal mawdu3` ("I had only just closed that subject"):
  idiomatic `ma sadda2t` + past, worth a native ear.
