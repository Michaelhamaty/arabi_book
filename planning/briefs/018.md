## Intent

Michael gave away something that wasn't his: Walid's closing hour, promised
to Dalia to sound like a man who decides. Sant Joan eve is the night it comes
due and the night Barcelona sets itself on fire. The chapter is about words
given in someone else's name, and the one name Michael asked to be called
by. It should be the book's hottest, loudest night, and it should cost.

The turn: Walid neither refuses (007) nor fights (016). A word that reached

word back or buy it off. Walid may draw one plain line about his name. Abu

**S02 — El sat7.** Dalia's yearly roof: cava in buckets, coca, the beach
below packed and roaring, fireworks along the coast, petards in the narrow
streets. Her guests are strangers and neighbors; she hires talkers so nobody
stands alone, and why is hers to reveal (her broke first year, fed on
Walid's credit, is available). Michael works the roof well: two or three
strangers, quick and specific. Dalia works at full tilt, leaving
mid-sentence. He tells her what Walid did with 21:30; she understands Walid
better than he does.

The turn: the person alone on the roof is the hostess, and Michael does his
job on her. The heat kept at a pavement's distance comes close: dancing,
hands, perhaps a kiss if both want it, her initiative as real as his. Before
anything happens he says there is someone and he doesn't know what they are;
Dalia decides what that means to her. The pay is handled openly, as
promised; she isn't buying the heat. Past midnight they may go down into the
crowd on the sand and back.

**Ending — in motion.** About 1:00, on the roof or the sand, Dalia starts a
dabke line of strangers and hands Michael its head. The chapter ends inside
the moving line, the city still exploding, with no verdict after it.
