## 004-S01 — 3al Zawiye, Friday evening

Proposed Arabizi title: *El rab7an ma byer7al* (the winner doesn't leave).

**Who is there:** Michael; **Abu Nabil** (about 70, Damascene); **Walid** (47,
Beiruti); **Em Jamil** (new, about 68, see below); a few other old men as
narrated background only, unnamed and with no lines. The café is full and loud,
as Abu Nabil promised. This is the first time the reader sees it that way.

**What each wants:**

- Michael wants one game, a little glory, and to leave on time. He also wants,
  without saying so, to be recognized here.
- Abu Nabil wants his rematch and an audience. He introduces Michael to the
  Friday crowd by the mars he gave him (a nickname like `Mars` or `el
  Amerkani` would suit him; he does not use Michael's name).
- Walid wants the machine to survive one Friday. He is theatrical, nosy, and
  wants to know where Michael is rushing off to.
- Em Jamil wants to play the winner. She doesn't care about anyone's dinner.

**Complication:** Michael **wins** against Abu Nabil, by luck and a big double
late in the game. Abu Nabil cries foul and announces a café law: the winner
doesn't leave the table until someone beats him. He claims the rule goes back to
the cafés of el Sham. Walid says loudly that the rule did not exist last week.
This is the first on-page display of Abu Nabil's maybe-invented stories. Michael
is trapped by his own victory. Em Jamil sits down. She is the surprise: the
quiet old woman among the old men, and the best player in the room. Abu Nabil
needles her with Homs jokes (`nukat el 7amasne`); she answers by winning. She
beats Michael without hurry, possibly by mars again.

The Friday rule is that losers pay for the table's food (callback: `Jib masari.
Mu lal la3be; lal akel.`). Michael pays. Meanwhile his phone has buzzed with
Inés's `wenak?`, and he has answered with some version of `jey`. Sami's build
message also lands here; keep both to one or two blockquote lines each.

**Turn:** When Michael finally stands to leave, Walid learns he is going to
dinner at a French girl's place, late, empty-handed. He is scandalized: nobody
walks into someone's home with empty hands. He packs a box of whatever the
Friday table was eating, marked with the café's name (a sticker or the green
paper), and won't take money for it. This is Walid's first act of care toward
Michael, given as an insult. The scene ends with Michael late, owing nothing to
the café, and holding something from it.

**Surprises (intentions, not dialogue):** Michael's win is the trap. Em Jamil
is lethal, not sweet. Abu Nabil's invented "law." The machine works only if
nobody mentions it, and someone mentions it. Walid's box.

## 004-S02 — Camille's dinner

Proposed Arabizi title: *El met2a55er* (the latecomer).

**Who is there:** Michael; **Inés** (24); **Camille** (22, French exchange
student, host); **Yara** (new, **22**, Beiruti, architecture student); and
**Jordi** (new, 27, Catalan, Camille's flatmate). Michael arrives very late,
between about 10:30 and 11, smelling of coffee and za3tar, with Walid's box.
Dinner is long over. Camille has saved his plate.

**What each wants:**

- Michael wants to be forgiven quickly and charmingly. His first instinct is
  to polish the story (as he did with Zeina in 003-S01). He tries "I got lost,"
  then "nobody in Spain eats before ten."
- **Camille** wants her party to keep its warmth. She is generous and amused,
  and the host who should be offended mostly isn't. Her small funny revenge:
  she is French, cooked for eight o'clock, and is proud of the dish that went
  cold (something homely and French; the writer chooses). She makes him eat it
  properly before anything from the box.
- **Jordi** wants someone to argue with about Barcelona. He defends Michael's
  "Spanish dinner time" excuse on principle, then corrects "Spain" to Catalunya,
  and asks whether Michael plans to learn Catalan as well as Spanish. This is a
  light political needle, not a lecture.
- **Inés** is amused and a little annoyed. She is not offended about the
  dinner. Her point is the word: her invitation had no obligation, but his
  `akid` was a promise, and "akid" means akid. She says it once, deadpan, and
  does not repeat it. Then she takes the box.
- **Yara** has her coat on or is about to leave when he walks in; she has a
  studio deadline (a physical model, a `maquette`) waiting upstairs, because she
  lives in the building. She stays "only to hear the excuse." She doesn't let
  any of it slide, even though it isn't her party, and says so: she wants to see
  what he does when caught.

**Complication:** Yara catches both excuses. "Lost," with a box from 3al Zawiye?
"Spanish time" at a French table? She recognizes the box: she has been to the
café once or twice, has opinions about Walid's coffee and his dying machine,
and doesn't know whether Walid would remember her. When Michael admits the truth
(two invitations, he said yes to both, he won and the winner couldn't leave),
she names the pattern more sharply than anyone has: if you say yes to everyone,
everyone gets half of you. This comes from a stranger, within the hour, and with
amusement rather than judgment. Michael answers calmly and with humor rather than
collapsing or over-apologizing. The banter is a real contest on both sides: she
tests, he holds his ground and tests back (her deadline, her opinions,
architecture), and each gets one point the other concedes.

**The disagreement:** Yara says places like 3al Zawiye survive on nostalgia:
old men, a sign, bad coffee, a museum of home. Michael says it is alive: people
come on Fridays, they play, they fight, they pay for each other's food. She
counters that he has been there **twice**. Neither wins.

**Turn and unfinished ending:** It becomes a bet. Whoever Walid greets by name
first wins. Neither is really a regular (he has been twice, she once or twice), so either
could lose.
The loser pays and concedes the argument. Michael asks for her number; she
refuses, because if he is really a regular, she'll find him there. No date or
time is set. She leaves for her maquette with the last word (perhaps turning his
`Ana jey` back on him). Michael, for once, does not promise anything. Close on
that, or on one brief beat with Inés afterwards. No walk home.

**Surprises (intentions, not dialogue):** the stranger, not the host, holds him
accountable. Jordi's defense backfires. The box exposes the lie. Yara knows the
café, and the bet is about the thing Michael most wants: to be known there.

## What changes by the end

1. **The first overcommitment has a cost.** Michael missed the dinner he
   promised, paid for the café's table, and spent Inés's trust a little. She now
   distinguishes his `akid` from his `jey`. He leaves owing her something
   unspoken.
2. **Michael meets Yara (22)**, the book's first flirtation (now in Barcelona).
   They part with a live bet and an unresolved argument about 3al Zawiye. **No
   numbers exchanged**; she has said she will find him at the café, without a
   date. The bet ties her to the place, not to his phone.
3. **3al Zawiye takes a step.** The Friday crowd now knows him, by nickname
   rather than name. Em Jamil has beaten him. Walid gave him the box. He is not
   a regular yet, and whether Walid knows his name has become a stake.
4. **Sami's build has arrived** and Michael has answered "tomorrow," the same
   word Walid curses.
