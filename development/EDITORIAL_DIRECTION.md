## What Chapter 004 demonstrates

### Consequences instead of another introduction

The double booking created in 003 becomes a choice with a cost. Michael sees the
clash, thinks he can handle it, and tells neither host. Winning traps him at the
board; Em Jamil's slow victory costs time and money. Inés says `Akid ya3ni akid`
once. Yara makes him admit the excuse is false. His interrupted `Ak—` to Jordi
and his refusal to promise at the exit are small changes, not a completed cure.

### Independent people and unequal rhythms

Abu Nabil wants an audience and invents laws; Walid wants his business to work;
Em Jamil wants to play and does not care about dinner. Camille wants her food
eaten, Jordi wants an argument, Inés wants words to mean something and her client
to pay, Yara has a maquette upstairs. Michael is involved in their lives without
being the reason all those lives exist.

Em Jamil speaks very little. Her `Allah ysam7o` and `Mars. Sa7tein.` do different
work from Abu Nabil's loud accusations, Walid's monologues, or Inés's flat reply.
Yara follows up, corners, concedes a point, and deflects her own slipped compliment.
Michael answers her; neither becomes an unquestioned winner or an emotional coach.

### Language does social work

Profanity at the board (`Yel3an abu hal zahr`, the stronger outburst `Ya ebn el
kalb`) belongs to this competitive setting and these relationships. Walid's
`bala tarbaye` and threatened broken hand accompany a gift. Em Jamil's endearments
are cutting rather than grandmotherly. Yara's `7abibi` can corner a man rather
than declare affection. These are examples of range, not a required phrase bank.
Do not make every person swear, make each scene louder, or sanitize the good lines.

### One object can change a scene

Walid's green box shows care, carries the café into a different circle, and
exposes Michael's lie. It is not choreography for its own sake. The contrast of
Michael's `bokra ... wallah` with Walid cursing his tomorrow-man is left for the
reader; narration does not explain the lesson. Skip unneeded travel and logistics.

### Reviews keep texture while checking facts

The language pass corrected marker bleed (Sami's `eza`, Abu Nabil's `hayda`,
Michael's `hniik` in the final story pass) without flattening everyone into one
voice. The story pass reconciled the 8/9 pm expectations, lengthened Em Jamil's
game to fit the evening, restored a skipped dice turn, and removed a food-tasting
gesture already used in 003. It kept profanity and two-sided banter.

## Repetition: the operational rule

**Do not repeat a conversation just because it fits the setting.** A callback is
worth keeping when its consequence, meaning, relationship, information, or stakes
change. Recurring vocabulary and speech habits are natural; copying the same
question-answer-joke-resolution pattern is not.

| Material already used | A new chapter must earn its return |
| --- | --- |
| Packing, luggage, keys, first greetings (001–003) | No second tour or repeated account of arriving; use a new practical or personal issue. |
| Zeina's arrival report, food/clothes banter, reciprocal-call agreement | Discuss something new or contact someone else for a real reason; don't redo the agreement. |
| Walid's machine, café dream, manual offer (003) | New stakes may justify a return. In 004 the ban/jinx, paid table, and gift did; a third identical breakdown-and-help offer would not. |
| Tawle loss (003), rematch and second mars (004) | No replay of the same game and insults in 005 merely to recreate 004's energy. |
| Michael's lateness, `akid`, yes-to-everyone confrontation (004) | Let changed behavior or a fresh cost speak. Don't make Rami or Karim deliver Yara's diagnosis again. |
| Café nostalgia/living-place debate, name bet, refused number (004) | Preserve the terms; don't renegotiate them or rerun the same flirtation elsewhere. |
| Jordi's Spain/Catalunya correction (004) | Karim's political/intellectual edge needs a different stake and individual argument, not the same nationality exchange. |

Before drafting, the planner lists the proposed conversations and checks 003–004
and `memory/COVERAGE.tsv` for the nearest earlier version. For each close match,
state the new turn or replace it. Reviewers cite specific similarities. Do not
impose a ban on familiar topics, a numerical novelty score, or a profanity quota.

## Spelling discipline without erasing dialect

1. Read `development/LEXICON.tsv` and the spelling table before writing. When
   adding a term, search settled chapters for its established spelling and sense.
2. Preserve a real voice difference (`mu`/`mesh`, `iza`/`eza`, `ya3ni`/`ya3ne`,
   `honik`/`hniik`, `leish`/`lesh`); do not normalize it mechanically.
3. Use `8` for the gh sound, `5` for kh, `2` for the glottal stop, and `q` when
   retained; keep `ra7`. Plain foreign-national speakers are not a license for
   English-shaped dialogue or a fake accent.
4. Read the whole source in addition to `check`/`stats`. Listed drift checks
   match known single tokens; the multiword `walla shi` entry is only a reviewer
   reminder. Passing is not proof that every word, phrase, or dialect choice is right.
5. Do not introduce new blocking lexicon entries that invalidate settled prose
   without an explicit reviewed correction. Never edit settled chapter text or
   refresh its hash as part of a documentation task.
